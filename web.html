Machines

Every AI machine on the tailnet. free: ready for work. working: on a task (number shown). busy: another program's model is loaded, so the fleet waits rather than push it out (a Story Writer book keeps its GPUs). down: asleep or not serving. Size: tiny ≤4B, small 7-8B, large 24-27B. Work starts on the smallest size that can do it.

Paid AI today

Calls to Claude, Gemini and Copilot through your own subscriptions, counted since midnight (Copilot also by month). Each one has its own daily limit; Raise gives one more calls for today only, with your OK (any number). Plan usage is each plan's own count, read without spending any of it: Copilot's from GitHub, Claude's from this machine's session logs (Google reports no Gemini quota without a request). It's read every 15 minutes. Each call needs your OK, and the OK carries over if one is locked out and the next one answers. A call that hit a usage lock isn't counted. Once every limit is reached or every one is locked, an approved task keeps your OK and runs when one frees up. The log at the bottom lists every call and lock.

Backlog

loading the AI board…

The AI board's open work in your order: product first (the ranks below), then the oldest item. Best AI is who should do it and why (an item you assigned keeps your choice). Assign hands it to that AI on the board, so its next session picks it up first. Plan asks the fleet for a written plan (an ordinary task in the Queue). Build makes the change: a new branch, the edit by Aider on a local model (free) or Claude Code with edit tools only (paid: waits for your OK), then a draft PR for you to review. Builds never touch main, never run the code they write (CI on the PR does), and a change over the size limit is refused. Queues: each AI has its own build queue and worker, one build at a time. Each build has a time estimate (Story Writer work in hours); a waiting build's time starts only when the one ahead of it finishes. A build is never stopped for taking long: well past its estimate it's flagged seems stalled for you to look at. When one AI is unavailable (Ollama not answering, a paid AI locked by its usage limit, switched off or out of calls), only its queue waits; the others keep building. Autopilot fills each queue by itself in this order, one build at a time per queue, up to its daily limit in all; Stop and Pause stop it too. The same order is on the claude.ai page "Backlog product priority".

Ask the fleet

Write the job and pick its kind. The kind decides which size of machine starts it. The answer appears in the queue.

Designs

Turn a finished idea (like #301's list of gaming accessories) into checked 3D parts. A local model lists every candidate part, one printed part per design; a product made of several parts gets an assembly row that waits for all its parts. Each part gets a brief (its purpose and size), then a build spec; structural parts are built in OpenSCAD by the 3D print tool and their size is checked against the brief (a wrong size is rebuilt, up to 3 tries). artistic parts wait for the image generator. When a part is ready to look at, check the picture: Approve, or Change and say what. Make a manual (an option) writes step-by-step build instructions for an approved part or a whole product. Approve saves the part on gpu-server in ~/designs/idea-<task>/<product>/<part>/ (STL, preview, spec, brief; the manual goes there too); each row says where, and Download (zip) gives you a part, a whole product, or (on the "parts of" row) the whole idea. All of it runs on local models; paid AI only with your OK.

Job watch checks

Routine checks, kept out of the queue so they don't bury your jobs. Each scheduled job (PR reviews, the Owner View feed, the nightly scans) is read by a local model after it runs. ok means its output looked normal; anything else is a problem, also reported on the board. Tap a row for the output the model read.

Models

Which model takes which kind of work. Scores (0 to 10) come from public reviews and benchmarks, read by a research task; each shows its source. None counts until you accept it. Without scores, each machine uses its own model and paid AI its usual one. Local, free models are always tried first; a machine only switches model when it's idle and the other model scores at least 2 higher. "ours" is how often that model's checked answers passed here (it counts from 20 tasks). New proposals: fleet.py scores propose.

Memory

Notes the fleet and your Claude sessions keep for later: answers, decisions, things to remember. Search finds notes by meaning (a local model on gpu-server indexes them) or, while no machine is free, by the words in them. Anything that looks like a password or key is never saved. Only you can delete a note.

How to use
  1. Send a job. In "Ask the fleet", pick summarize, paste the text, press Send. Within a minute a free small machine takes it; its row in the Queue turns finished. Tap the row to read the answer.
  2. Review code. Pick review, write what to look for (for example "Security and coding-standard problems, file by file"), then under Code to read pick the checkout and either some files or folders, or a PR number. The files go to the machine with the request; a big checkout or PR is split across machines and the answers merged.
  3. A careful job. Pick review, tick "Have another model check it". A 27B machine answers, the other 27B checks. If the check fails, the step log says why and the job moves up.
  4. Paid AI. Pick hard (or tick "May ask for paid AI"). The task appears under "Waiting for your OK" with the reason. Press Approve to send it to Claude once, or Deny. "Paid AI today" shows what's left of the 10 a day.
  5. Stop everything paid. Press Stop paid AI at the top. Nothing goes to Claude, Gemini or Copilot until you press it again. Local machines carry on.
  6. A paid AI is "locked"? It used up its plan's usage (Claude's 5-hour window, Gemini's day, Copilot's month). Approved tasks go to the next one at once: Claude, then Gemini, then Copilot, which is only used when both others can't (10 a month). When all are locked the task waits with your OK kept and runs when one frees up. Unlock clears a lock early.
  7. A long job. Write what to do (for example "List every error and its time"), tick Split across machines and paste the long text. Free machines answer the parts at the same time; the row shows "3 of 8 parts done", then one 27B machine merges the answers.
  8. Cancel one task. Tap its row in the Queue, then Cancel task. If a machine is already working on it, the machine finishes but the answer is thrown away.
  9. Pause everything. Press Pause all at the top: no machine starts anything new, tasks already running finish, and the queue keeps the rest. Press Resume to carry on.
  10. "moved up ×2"? A check failed, so the task went to a bigger machine. Hover the pill (or tap the row) to see why.
  11. Find a note. In Memory, type what you're after in your own words and press Search. Each note shows where it came from and when; press Delete to remove one. Claude sessions save and search notes too.
  12. Research the web. Pick research, write the question, and put the pages to read under Web pages to read, one per line. Each page is screened first: text aimed at the AI is cut and shows under Screen hits. The answer says which page each point came from.
  13. A machine is down? It's asleep or not serving; the fleet skips it and uses the others. Its work goes back to the queue after 30 minutes if it went quiet mid-task.

From the terminal on gpu-server: fleet.py submit summarize "text", fleet.py submit review "what to check" --repo my-app --path src/api.py (or --pr 122; fleet.py repos lists checkouts), fleet.py status 12, fleet.py approve 12. If this page is down: fleet.py top shows the same on one terminal screen and can approve, cancel, pause and stop. Details: RUNBOOK.md.
